Why "Aim for 2% Keyword Density" Is Outdated SEO Advice

Published 2026-09-14

Where the advice came from

In search engines' earlier years, ranking algorithms relied much more heavily on simple keyword matching — counting how often a search term appeared on a page was a meaningful signal of relevance. This gave rise to specific numeric density targets, like the once-common advice to aim for roughly 1-2% keyword density.

Why that stopped being good advice

Modern search engines use vastly more sophisticated language understanding to judge what a page is actually about — synonyms, related concepts, and overall topical coverage all matter far more than literally repeating one exact phrase a specific number of times. Deliberately repeating a phrase to hit a percentage target (a practice called keyword stuffing) can now actively hurt a page, since it reads unnaturally and search engines are specifically designed to recognize and penalize it.

What to actually check instead

Rather than targeting a density number, a word-frequency check is more useful for catching accidental problems: a keyword that never appears at all (worth checking you actually mentioned your main topic), or one word that's repeated so often it makes the writing feel robotic and repetitive to a human reader — both are worth catching, neither requires hitting a specific percentage.
