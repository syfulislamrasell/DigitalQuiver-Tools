Why Google Sometimes Rewrites Your Meta Description Anyway

Published 2026-09-14

A meta description is a suggestion, not a guarantee

A page's meta description tag is written specifically to suggest what should appear as the snippet text under its title in search results. Many site owners assume that whatever they write there is exactly what will show up — but Google has always treated it as a strong suggestion, not a binding instruction.

When Google substitutes its own text

Google's own documentation confirms it will replace a page's provided meta description with text pulled directly from the page's content when it judges that alternative to be more relevant to a specific search query. This means the same page can show different snippet text depending on what someone actually searched for.

Why this doesn't make writing one pointless

Even though Google won't always use it verbatim, a well-written meta description still matters: it's what displays for many queries, it's a natural place to include a clear call to action, and a genuinely bad or missing one increases the odds Google pulls an awkward, out-of-context sentence from your page instead.
