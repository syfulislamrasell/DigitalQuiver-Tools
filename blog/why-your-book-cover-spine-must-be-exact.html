Why Your Book Cover's Spine Width Has to Be Exact, Not Approximate

Published 2026-09-14

The spine isn't a fixed size

Unlike an eBook cover, which is just a flat rectangle, a paperback cover is one continuous file that wraps around the front cover, spine, and back cover. The spine's width isn't a standard size — it depends entirely on how thick the finished, printed book will be, which in turn depends on page count and which paper stock you choose.

The formula: page count × paper thickness

Each page type has a fixed thickness Amazon uses to calculate total spine width: white paper is thinner (roughly 0.002252 inches per page) than cream paper (roughly 0.0025 inches per page), because cream stock is a heavier paper. Color-ink options add further variation depending on whether you choose standard or premium color printing. A 300-page book prints noticeably thicker on cream paper than on white, purely from the paper choice — not the page count.

Why getting this wrong causes real problems

If your cover template assumes a spine width that doesn't match your actual final page count and paper choice, the spine text can end up wrapping onto the front or back cover, or leaving an awkward gap — either of which typically gets flagged during Amazon's automated cover review, delaying your publication.

A word of caution on exact constants

The per-page thickness constants are useful for early planning and rough drafts of your cover design, but different publishing resources cite slightly different values for some paper types (particularly premium color). Before submitting your final print-ready file, always run your exact page count through Amazon's own official KDP Cover Calculator — it reflects Amazon's live, current formula and is the authoritative source your file will actually be checked against.
