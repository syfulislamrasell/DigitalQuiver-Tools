Does the Meta Keywords Tag Still Matter for SEO? (No — Here's Why)

Published 2026-09-14

It used to genuinely matter

In the early days of search engines, the <meta name="keywords"> tag was a real, functioning ranking signal — site owners would list the terms they wanted to rank for, and search engines gave that list real weight when deciding relevance. That mattered enough that it created an obvious incentive to abuse it.

Why it stopped working

Because the meta keywords tag was entirely self-reported by the website itself, with no way to verify it reflected the page's actual content, it became one of the most heavily abused SEO tools of the early web — site owners would stuff it with dozens of popular, sometimes entirely unrelated, high-traffic terms purely to attract search visibility. Google publicly confirmed in 2009 that it does not use the meta keywords tag in its web search ranking at all, precisely because the abuse had made it useless as a genuine relevance signal.

What replaced it

Modern search ranking relies on actually analyzing a page's real content, structure, and how other pages link to it, rather than trusting a self-declared list of keywords. Structured data (like the Article and WebApplication schema markup used across this site) plays a role in how search engines understand a page's content and can enable enhanced search result features, but it works by describing what's genuinely on the page, not by claiming keywords unrelated to it.

What still genuinely matters in your meta tags

  • The title tag is still a meaningful, direct ranking factor and is usually what's shown as the clickable headline in search results.
  • The meta description isn't a direct ranking factor, but it strongly influences whether someone actually clicks your result once they see it — effectively functioning as ad copy for your page in the results list.
  • Open Graph and Twitter Card tags control how your page looks when shared on social media, which has nothing to do with search ranking but meaningfully affects social click-through.
